abstract: >-
  Fourier-Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy is a rapid technique for chemical characterisation of materials. This study develops machine-learning models to link the chemical characterisation of asphalt binders from FTIR with their rheological properties that are critical to pavement performance. Emphasis is placed on training models with generalizability across different binder types. A nationwide asphalt binder database was compiled to support model development. The proposed framework incorporates a feature extraction module that condenses high-dimensional FTIR data into key latent features, and a regression module that maps these latent features to rheological properties. Results show that the deep-learning model architecture–autoencoder for feature extraction and multilayer perceptron for regression–achieves higher prediction accuracy compared to traditional methods based on partial least square or FTIR peak analysis. Interpretation of the machine-learning models reveals key FTIR spectral features that significantly influence the rheological behaviour of binders. The models hold potential for rapid quality assessment of asphalt binders in pavement engineering.
